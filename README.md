# Data analysis on Public Spending on Environmental Initiatives 
Background:While previous studies have attempted to explain the relationship between different identities and attitudes towards climate challenges and sustainability initiatives, most of these studies have focused on market behaviors and spending on a household consumer level. This approach overlooks the complexity of citizens' identities, the relationships they have with other variables, and the variables that go beyond traditional demographic profiles that could be more indicative of their socio-economic and political environments. This study aims to fill the gaps in previous research by exploring the factors that influence citizens' attitudes towards environmental initiatives, including having children and confidence in institutions. By identifying significant demographic and attitudinal variables that predict the likelihood of support for spending on environmental initiatives, this study seeks to provide insights that can inform more effective communication and policy strategies to promote and accelerate public environmental action. 

Summary of analysis results: 
Traditional demographic data might not be the most predictive indicator of people’s attitude towards public investment in environmental initiatives. Instead, the analysis suggests that people's relationship with social institutions has a more significant impact on their attitude towards improving environmental infrastructure. This raises questions about the increasingly crucial role of social institutions in shaping public opinion on climate change- related issues. These findings also have implications for climate communication strategies, as it emphasizes the need to consider the influence of people's relationships with institutions when designing and delivering messages.
